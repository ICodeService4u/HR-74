# Payroll Schedule

Quick reference for how we pay everyone at Troutly. If you're new, read this first — it answers most of the questions we get.

## When we pay

We run payroll semi-monthly. That means checks land on the **15th** and the **last day** of each month. If either date falls on a weekend or a bank holiday, the check moves to the business day just before.

That works out to **24 checks** across the year — twelve mid-month runs and twelve end-of-month runs.

## How salaried pay is calculated

For anyone on salary, each check is **annual salary / 24**. Same amount every time, no matter how many workdays fell inside the pay period. Simple, predictable, and it's what the offer letters point to.

## Timesheets

Hourly folks and contractors: get your timesheet in **two days before the pay date**. That gives us time to review, reconcile with the manager, and cut the check on time. Late timesheets roll to the next run — we don't do off-cycle for missed submissions.

Managers, please approve on the same two-day window. If you're going to be out, delegate.

## New hires

If your start date lands mid-period, we pay you from your start date forward on a **calendar-day** basis. We count actual days in the period (including weekends), divide, and apply that fraction to the per-check amount. First check may look small — that's why.

Same math runs in reverse if someone separates mid-period.

## PTO, expenses, one-offs

- PTO used shows up on the check for the period it was taken.
- Approved reimbursed expenses submitted by the timesheet deadline ride along with the same check.
- Bonuses and commissions run through the normal payroll on the pay date closest to when they're approved.

## Common questions

**"Why don't the two halves of the month have the same number of workdays?"** They don't need to — we're paying by the calendar half-month, not by hours worked. Your check is the same either way.

**"What if the 15th is a Saturday?"** Check drops on Friday the 14th.

**"Can I get paid early?"** No. Off-cycle checks are reserved for corrections and true emergencies, and Finance has to approve.
