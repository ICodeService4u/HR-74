# Compensation Authority

*Who can change pay at Troutly.*

This page is informational. The controlling documents are the Employee Handbook (sections on compensation and pay administration) and the current Compensation Bands Memo issued by Finance. If anything here drifts from those, they win.

## Who signs off on base salary

Base salary changes require a written approval signed by the employee's manager and the CEO or CTO. Both signatures, on the same document, before the change is entered anywhere. No exceptions for market adjustments, retention, mid-cycle raises, or anything else that touches the annual number.

That means:

- A manager alone can't move a salary.
- A CEO or CTO alone can't move a salary.
- People Ops can prepare the paperwork, but we don't approve it.
- Once both signatures are on the document, we route it to payroll and update the HRIS record.

## When the system and the paper disagree

Where the HRIS and a signed compensation document disagree, the signed document governs until the record is corrected. Pay the person from the signed doc, then fix the system. If you find one of these mismatches, flag it to People Ops so we can open a correction ticket and get the HRIS back in line.

This mirrors Handbook v3 section 3: the HRIS is our system of record, but a signed comp document outranks it whenever they conflict.

## Support Specialist step increases

Support Specialists receive an automatic $3,000.00 annual step increase on each service anniversary. This is triggered by the anniversary date, not by a manager review — no approval memo, no performance check, no separate signature required. The step is baked into the role.

Practically:

- On the anniversary date, the new annual salary is old annual salary + $3,000.00.
- The increase takes effect in the pay period containing the anniversary.
- The first paycheck at the new rate is the first regular biweekly payroll on or after the effective date.
- People Ops watches for upcoming anniversaries and updates the record; no ticket needed from the manager.

## Sign-on bonuses

A sign-on bonus pays on the first regular payroll after the start date. Not the start date itself, not a special off-cycle check — the next scheduled biweekly run. The amount is whatever the signed offer letter specifies.

If a new hire's start date shifts, the bonus follows the actual start date, not the original one.

## What this page does not cover

- **Bands and midpoints:** see the current Compensation Bands Memo from Finance.
- **Promotions and level changes:** same signature rule as base pay — manager + CEO or CTO — plus a leveling note.
- **Bonus plans (variable comp, commissions):** governed by the plan document, not this page.
- **Contractor rates:** contractor agreements, not employee compensation policy.
