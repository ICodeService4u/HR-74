# Paid Time Off

This is the quick-reference page for how we do PTO at Troutly. If you want the full legal-y version, grab the policy document linked at the bottom.

## The basics

We accrue PTO **monthly**, posted on the 1st of each month. Your monthly grant equals your **annual tier divided by 12**, dropped into your balance on the first day of the month. Nothing fancy — the whole month's chunk lands on day one and it's yours to use.

There's no waiting period. If you're here on the 1st, you accrue on the 1st.

## Tiers

Your annual tier is based on how long you've been with us (adjusted service date, if you've been rehired):

- **Under 2 years of service:** 80 hours per year
- **2 years up to (but not including) 5 years:** 120 hours per year
- **5 years and over:** 160 hours per year

You tier up in the pay period that contains your service anniversary — the next monthly accrual after that anniversary comes in at the new rate.

## Carryover

Whatever you don't use rolls forward. Balances **carry over without limit** from year to year. We don't zero anyone out at year-end and we don't cap what you can bank. Take the time you need; if you don't need it, keep it.

## What a balance is worth

Your balance is measured in hours, and hours are valued at your **current hourly rate** — meaning your annual pay rate on the measurement date divided by 2,080. If your salary goes up, the dollar value of your balance goes up with it. If you take a vacation day, the day is paid at whatever your rate is when you take it.

## Requesting time off

Talk to your manager first. Once you've agreed on the dates, log the time off in the HR system so payroll and your team know you're out. We don't require a specific amount of advance notice — just be reasonable and give your team enough runway to cover for you.

## Payout at separation

If you leave Troutly, unused PTO is paid out only if you give at least 14 calendar days of written notice and work through that notice period. Give proper notice and work it out, and your remaining balance gets cashed out on your final check at your current hourly rate. Skip the notice, walk early, or get terminated for cause and no payout is owed.

This one's important — Texas doesn't require employers to pay unused leave at separation unless the written policy promises it, and ours promises it only under those two conditions.

## Rehires

If you leave and come back, your prior service bridges as long as your break was less than 365 days. That means your tier picks up where it left off and your adjusted service date is your original start date. If the break was a year or longer, the clock resets to your rehire date.

## FAQ

**Do PTO hours expire?** No. Balances carry over without limit.

**Can I take PTO before I've accrued it?** Talk to your manager. We can front-load in reasonable cases, but if you leave with a negative balance we'll square up on the final check.

**Does the company observe holidays separately?** Yes — the office holiday calendar is separate from PTO and doesn't draw down your balance.

**What about sick time?** Use PTO. We don't run a separate sick bucket.

**Who do I ask if I have questions?** Ping the People Ops channel or email the Office Manager.
